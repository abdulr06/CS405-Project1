Part ATransformations 20 points

Write your own 4×4 matrix library — translation, rotation, scaling — and show that the order of multiplication changes the result. Matrices are column-major, exactly as WebGPU expects them in a uniform buffer.

M = T · R · S (scale first — the usual choice)

      

      

        
M = S · R · T (scale last — scales the translation too)

        
        

      

    

  

  

    

      
What you implement

      
    
        
  • mat4: identity, multiply, translate, rotate (x, y, z), scale, transpose, inverse of a rigid transform
    • 
        
  • An object whose translation, rotation and scale you can change from the UI
    • 
        
  • A switch between T·R·S and S·R·T, with both matrices shown on screen
    • 
        
  • No matrix library (no gl-matrix, no Three.js): the maths is the assignment
    • 
      

    

    

      
Report

      
Explain in one paragraph, with the two matrices side by side, why the results differ, and state which order you use everywhere else in the project and why.

    

  






  
Part BScene graph 15 points

  
Objects are rarely independent. Build a small tree in which every node holds a local transform, and a node's world matrix is the product of the matrices along the path from the root.

  

    

      

        
      

      

        

      

    

  

  

    

      
What you implement

      
    
        
  • A node type with: local transform, children, and a computed world matrix
    • 
        
  • At least three levels of hierarchy and at least five nodes
    • 
        
  • One animated joint so the hierarchy is visible in motion
    • 
        
  • Per-node axes drawn on request (useful for debugging, and for your report)

Tie to the painting

      
Build something the painting suggests: objects standing on the painted table, a lamp arm above it, a mobile hanging over a still life. The content is free; the hierarchy is not.

    

  






  
Part CCamera and projection 20 points

  
Derive the view matrix from an eye, a target and an up vector, then project with a perspective or an orthographic matrix. The frustum in the left panel is what the camera can actually see.

  

    

      

        
World view — your camera drawn as a frustum

        
      

      

        
What that camera sees

        
      

    

    

  

  

    

      
What you implement

      
    
        
  • lookAt(eye, target, up) built from two cross products
    • 
        
  • perspective(fov, aspect, near, far) mapping depth into z ∈ [0, 1] (WebGPU, not OpenGL)
    • 
        
  • orthographic(...) and a switch between the two
    • 
        
  • Orbit controls with the mouse; the camera parameters shown on screen
    • 
        
  • A drawing of your own frustum, seen from a second camera
    • 
      

    

    

      
Watch out

      
    
        
  • If up is parallel to the view direction, the cross product is zero and the camera flips. Handle it.
    • 
        
  • Near too small or far too large wastes depth precision — say what you chose in the report.
    • 
        
  • Perspective divide happens after the matrix: ndc = clip.xyz / clip.w.
    • 
      

    

  






  
Part DCurves over the painting 25 points

  
Trace a shape from your painting with control points, evaluate the curve yourself, and send an object along it. The painting is a textured quad in the scene — the starter code gives you that quad.

  

  

    

      
What you implement

      
    
        
  • Cubic Bézier evaluation, and the de Casteljau construction drawn for a chosen t
    • 
        
  • A piecewise curve with at least three segments and C¹ continuity at the joints
    • 
        
  • One of: B-spline basis evaluation, or a Catmull–Rom spline through the points
    • 
        
  • Control points placed and dragged with the mouse, in the plane of the painting
    • 
        
  • An object animated along the curve, with its orientation following the tangent
    • 
        
  • Export the curve as JSON (points in normalised coordinates)
    • 
      

    

    

      
Report

      
Show the Bézier basis functions, prove that the curve starts at P₀ and ends at P₃, and state the condition for C¹ continuity at a joint. Include a screenshot of your traced shape over the painting.
